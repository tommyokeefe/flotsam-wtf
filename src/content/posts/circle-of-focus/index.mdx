In last week's ["TFW My Circle of Concern Is Actually The Entire Universe"](https://www.flotsam.wtf/posts/circle-of-concern) post I called out a fairly acute problem I've been dealing with and mentioned something I've found to be a pretty good working solution. I want to spend some time expanding on that solution because I think it's key to effectively taking a proactive approach to work and life in the current time and place we find ourselves in culturally (at least for big feelers like myself... I can't really speak for everyone else).

The problem I called out was this: 

> One of the things I've realized is somewhere along the way I equated accepting the fact that I don't have any control or influence over some things with being okay with the state of those things. I can rage at injustice and inequity and still accept that I cannot change them. In reality not accepting that means I don't have any energy left to focus on the stuff I can control and influence.

To state it bluntly: if my circle of concern contains everything, I can't really differentiate between what is in my control or out of my control, let alone the nuanced places where I may have influence. The three circles Covey calls out (concern, influence, control) get blown away and I'm left stuck in a reactive place. 

The working solution I named was fairly straight forward: 

> So for me, things actually start with redrawing my map. Instead of a circle of concern I'm starting with a bounded circle of focus. Where does my focus start? On me. Where does it extend? To those in closest proximity to me. What time frame am I most concerned with? Right now and the immediate future. Focus. Accept what I can't change outside of that. Accept what I can't change inside as well. Then I'm ready to move forward. 

Remap. Redraw. Refocus. Pick a narrow slice. 

In the movie "Everything Everywhere All At Once" they nail so much of what I I mean when I say "my circle of concern is actually the entire universe". In that movie the subject matter is literally everything, the location is everywhere in the broadest multi-verse sense possible, and the time frame is all at once. The overwhelm of life is so real. By the end of the movie the character I identify with the most is Waymond Wang. I often come back to his speech during the climactic moment of the final act. Everyone is fighting, and Waymond gives this heart felt speech that is really more of a plea for peace than anything else. It's split between two different Waymond's and the whole thing is excellent, but the line I always come back to is this one: 

> When I choose to see the good side of things, I'm not being naive. It is strategic and necessary. It's how I've learned to survive through everything. — Waymond Wang

That's the starting point for me. It's the first move in creating a circle of focus. A strategic and necessary move that says "no matter how wild shit is right now, there is still goodness and beauty, and I still have the ability to be a part of it." That move sets me up for an actually proactive stance. 

Expanding on this further, once you have your orientation (a strategic and necessary focus on the good), how we choose to narrow matters. Rather than choosing one of the many dimensions of life you can filter these things through (and there are many, the facets feel almost infinite) start with RIGHT HERE and RIGHT NOW and pick the thing immediately in front of you. What is it? Examine it. Examine your reaction to it. Are you excited by it? Scared of it? Dreading it? Interrogate your emotional reaction to it. Is it something you control? Great, let's talk about the next small meaningful step you can take. Is it something you can't control? Set it aside for now. We'll talk more about the difference between "no control at all" and "inside my sphere of influence" later, but for now just let it go. The key to being proactive is finding the next meaningful step and doing it. 

If I pull this out from the moment to the "what will I accomplish today" or "what will I accomplish this week" or "what do I want to do this month", etc... the general principle holds. I don't start with the universe of things I could consider. I start with that time box, and a specific place or arena, IE "my job" or "my personal growth" or "my family". That becomes the frame for "RIGHT HERE" and "RIGHT NOW". You start by limiting the circle of concern drastically using time and place. That's the "circle of focus". Maybe this is too basic, but for me, I know my tendency is always one of expansive outlook. My risk is being paralyzed into reactivity by the sheer volume of information (a lot of it terrifying in one way or another) that is constantly assaulting me. Even if your risk is different, and you're real tendency is to miss the forest for one very particular tree, a well tuned circle of focus could be of great use. If your starting frame is a week 9and I think it's a good place to start) you can march through a few different areas of focus — family & friends, relationship, your personal growth, work, maybe even a specific volunteer opportunity — bounded by that week and come up with a good list of things that you can then prioritize and spread out into a reasonable plan for the rest of the week. You're no being reactive, either stuck in a paralyzed position or myopically fixated on one particular piece. Instead, you're actively choosing where to spend your energy. 

Give it a try. Next time you go to plan your week, or even just your day, start with that clear circle of focus and use that as a context for what you control and what you don't. I know it makes a difference for me. I go from desperate versions of "GRANT ME SERENITY!!!" to "I need the wisdom to discern what I control and what I don't". 

Have a little bit of Waymond Wang if you're having trouble getting started.
